Peer Review ReportTitle: Logarithmic boundary depth and the conditioning of the discrete inverse conductance problem on hyperbolic latticesAuthor: Xavier Callens1. Overall AssessmentThis is an exceptionally rigorous, original, and well-written manuscript. The author explores how the underlying geometry of a discrete graph affects the conditioning of the inverse conductance (Calderón) problem. The core finding—that the negative curvature of hyperbolic lattices keeps all nodes within $O(\log N)$ steps of the boundary, thereby circumventing the exponential conditioning collapse seen in flat $O(\sqrt{N})$ lattices—is elegant, physically intuitive, and convincingly supported by numerical and analytical evidence.The methodological maturity of this paper is outstanding. The integration of preregistered hypotheses, exact rank certificates over finite fields to separate numerical conditioning from algebraic identifiability, and the cross-validation of RC dynamics using two distinct ODE integrators establishes a gold standard for computational reproducibility. The manuscript is highly suitable for publication, requiring only minor-to-moderate revisions to address constraints surrounding the flat-lattice scaling limits and the background conductance assumptions.2. Key StrengthsDisentangling Identifiability and Conditioning: The use of exact algebraic rank computations over $\mathbb{F}_p$ is brilliant. It definitively proves that flat networks are algebraically identifiable and that their failure is strictly a numerical limitation of floating-point precision, rather than a fundamental theoretical loss of information.Preregistration and Open Science: Explicitly documenting a refuted hypothesis (the constant Dirichlet spectral gap) and subsequently proving the correct asymptotic behavior via spectral graph theory (Proposition 1) builds immense trust in the scientific rigor of this work. The cryptographically verified claim ledger is an exemplary practice.Robust Software Engineering: Cross-validating the time-domain model with SciPy and rusty-SUNDIALS, while accurately mapping dynamic limits to the static Dirichlet-to-Neumann problem, bridges theoretical mathematics to tabletop hardware physics flawlessly.Geometric Insight: The explicit linkage of the condition number to the graph's maximal boundary depth ($d_{max}$) provides a highly intuitive physical mechanism for the observed phenomena.3. Major Critiques & Areas for ImprovementA. The "Flat Lattice" Scaling Law is Incomplete (Limitation i)As acknowledged in Section 4, the flat-lattice scaling relies on only three unsaturated data points before IEEE double precision (64-bit float64) fails. Consequently, the paper cannot distinguish between an exponential scaling $\kappa \sim e^{c\sqrt{N}}$ and a steep power law. Because the analytical formulas for the Jacobian (Eqs. 1 & 2) only rely on exact combinatorial properties, this limitation is purely a hardware/datatype artifact. Leaving this unresolved slightly weakens the structural conclusions, as the exact asymptotic nature of the flat baseline is left unknown.B. Assumption of Unit Conductances (Limitation ii)The Jacobian conditioning $\kappa$ is evaluated entirely at a homogeneous unit conductance state ($g_e = 1$). In real electrical impedance tomography (EIT) or physical resistor networks, the goal is often to detect anomalies or handle inhomogeneous backgrounds. It is not empirically shown if the $O(\log N)$ hyperbolic advantage persists when conductances vary by orders of magnitude (which typically worsens the condition number of the Laplacian).C. Probe-Matched Control Metric DiscrepanciesWhen subsampling the hyperbolic boundary to match flat-lattice probe counts, the author uses a post hoc metric (conditioning on the identifiable subspace) to bypass singular Jacobians caused by unmeasured degree-two nodes. While mathematically sound, dropping singular values means the two geometries are no longer recovering spaces of the same dimension. Comparing a 306-dimensional hyperbolic target to a 592-dimensional flat target slightly confounds geometry with dimensionality, making the $\kappa$ comparison an "apples-to-oranges" evaluation.4. Specific Recommendations for the Author1. Use Arbitrary-Precision Arithmetic to Resolve Flat Scaling (Highly Recommended)To resolve limitation (i), I strongly recommend using a multiple-precision mathematical library (e.g., Python's mpmath, Julia's BigFloat, or C's Arb) to compute the SVD of the Jacobian for the numerically singular flat lattices (e.g., $N=421, 797, 1069$). Computing the exact condition number beyond the $\sim 10^{16}$ double-precision limit for a few matrices of this size is computationally inexpensive for a one-off decomposition. This will definitively answer whether flat-lattice conditioning grows exponentially with $\sqrt{N}$ and complete Figure 1.2. Add a Small Perturbation / Inhomogeneous Test CaseTo address limitation (ii), add a brief numerical experiment evaluating $\kappa$ for a non-homogeneous case. For example, assign conductances drawn from a uniform distribution (e.g., $g_e \sim \mathcal{U}[0.5, 1.5]$) or introduce a localized high-contrast "defect." Adding a column to Table 1 or a brief scatter plot confirming that the hyperbolic conditioning advantage survives under these disordered conditions will significantly strengthen the paper's physical applicability to realistic EIT scenarios.3. Clarify the Dimensional Disparity in the Probe-Matched ControlIn Section 3.3, explicitly state that the physical loss of resolution (recovering only 306 combinations instead of 592 individual conductances) is an inherent tradeoff. Briefly add a sentence discussing whether the $10^{6.7}$ gap in the condition number is mathematically large enough to rule out dimensionality reduction as the sole driver of the improved conditioning.4. Expand on Proposition 1's "Argued, not machine-checked" StepIn Section 3.5, you note: "For general L the hypothesis follows from the face-incidence definition of the boundary; this step is argued, not machine-checked." Please add 1–2 sentences explicitly detailing this topological argument. Briefly explain geometrically why layer-truncating this specific tessellation based on face-incidence strictly guarantees that no interior node can lose an edge (i.e., verifying the degree-three interior bound).5. ConclusionThis is a mathematically elegant and phenomenally rigorous paper that brings fresh geometric intuition to discrete inverse problems. Addressing the flat lattice precision limits via arbitrary-precision solvers and including a brief heterogeneous test will elevate it from an excellent proof-of-concept to a definitive foundational text. I highly recommend acceptance following these minor-to-moderate revisions.
